About

Why this exists, how it works, and where the numbers come from.

WHY I BUILT THIS

I got into fantasy football rankings the way a lot of people probably do — reading article after article, comparing expert rankings, watching draft-prep videos, listening to podcasts on my commute. Somewhere in there I realized I didn't just want to read other people's rankings anymore. I wanted my own.

So I tried to build them myself, and immediately hit a wall: staring at a spreadsheet of 150+ names and trying to slot each one into an exact rank, all at once, is genuinely overwhelming. It's easy to lose the thread of what you actually believe when you're juggling that many players at the same time.

What I noticed, though, was that comparing just two players and picking the one I'd rather have was easy. Obvious, even, most of the time. That's the entire idea behind this tool: instead of ranking everyone at once, you make a long string of simple head-to-head calls, and the full ranking builds itself underneath you. No spreadsheet paralysis — just one decision at a time.

HOW IT WORKS

Every ranking on this site comes from the same core idea: you're shown two players, you pick the one you'd rather have, and the tool uses your answer to slot the winner into the right spot in your growing list — without needing to ask you about every single player you've already ranked.

A few things happen underneath that to make the rankings more useful:

  • Real stats, not guesses. Every player's numbers come from actual play-by-play NFL data, not estimates.
  • Live rosters. The player pool pulls current team and roster info so trades, cuts, and new additions stay reflected.
  • Rookie seeding. Players without NFL stats yet (rookies, or anyone who missed last season) get placed into the list based on how similarly-drafted players have historically performed — so a first-round pick doesn't get buried at the bottom by default.
  • Matchup-adjusted weekly mode. If you rank for a specific week instead of the full season, the tool factors in how tough that week's opponent has been against that position.

None of this replaces your own judgment — it just makes sure the starting point going into each comparison is a reasonable one.
